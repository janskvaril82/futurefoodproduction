Key idea

The novel-food test is about history and process, not about risk: a food is novel if it was not eaten to a significant degree in the EU before 15 May 1997 and belongs to one of ten categories. Risk enters only afterwards, in EFSA's assessment. That is why a lettuce from a vertical farm is not novel while a vitamin-D-enriched mushroom is.

Five questions the regulation asks

Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 can be read as a sequence of questions, and the tree follows that sequence. The order matters: a question further down only arises when the answers above lead there.

1. Is the product within the scope of the Regulation?

Article 2(2) excludes genetically modified food within the scope of Regulation (EC) No 1829/2003, and foods "when and in so far as" they are used as food enzymes, food additives, food flavourings or extraction solvents. These have their own pre-market procedures — for additives, enzymes and flavourings the common authorisation procedure of Regulation (EC) No 1331/2008. Stevia shows how one plant can fall under different regimes: steviol glycosides used as a sweetener are the additive E 960 (Regulation (EU) No 1131/2011), the dried leaves were refused as a novel food in 2000, and in 2023 a simple infusion of the leaves was found not novel.

Precision fermentation sits on a boundary. A protein produced with a genetically modified micro-organism, from which the organism has been removed so that no GM material remains, is not a GM food; it is assessed as a novel food. If viable GM cells or recombinant DNA remained, Regulation 1829/2003 would apply instead (see Lesson 14.2).

2. Was it eaten to a significant degree in the EU before 15 May 1997?

This is the historical test of Article 3(2)(a). Consumption in any Member State counts, even one that joined the EU later. "Significant degree" is not defined numerically; evidence of sale to consumers, recipes, trade statistics or surveys counts, occasional or medicinal use does not. Use only in food supplements counts only for supplements (category (x)). When the evidence is unclear, the operator must consult the Member State where it first intends to market the food (Article 4; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2018/456): a decision within four months of validity, extendable once by at most four months. The Commission publishes every outcome — a treasure of precedents: mycoprotein from Fusarium venenatum strain A 3/5 not novel, the same species' strain NRRL 26228 novel; hydroponically grown tiger nuts not novel; UV-treated skimmed milk not novel.

Common misconception

"Food from a vertical farm or aquaponic system is novel because the production method is new." Category (vii) requires a process that significantly changes composition or structure, affecting nutritional value, metabolism or undesirable substances. Hydroponic lettuce is not novel. But a new species grown in a vertical farm can be: fresh Wolffia plants needed authorisation as a traditional food, with limits for heavy metals, microcystins and trace elements from fertilisers written into their specification.

4. Is it already on the Union list?

Authorisations are generic: once a novel food is on the Union list (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2470 and its amendments), anyone may market it within the authorised specification, conditions of use and labelling (Article 6(2)). The exception is data protection (Articles 26–27): if the applicant's proprietary data were indispensable for the assessment, for five years only that applicant may market the food, unless a competitor obtains its own authorisation without those data. Dried mealworm was protected until 22 June 2026; partially defatted house-cricket powder is protected until 24 January 2028.

5. Application or notification?

Every novel food can follow the application route (Articles 10–12): validity check, EFSA risk assessment of nine months net of clock stops, a draft implementing act within seven months of the opinion's publication and a qualified-majority vote of the Member States. A traditional food from a third country — derived from primary production, with at least 25 years of safe use in the customary diet of a significant number of people in at least one third country, and not in categories (i), (iii), (vii), (viii), (ix) or (x) — may instead be notified (Articles 14–15). Without reasoned safety objections within four months, the Commission authorises it without a committee vote. The price of speed: no data protection (Article 26(3)).

What the dossier contains

An application consists of a cover letter, a technical dossier and a summary (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2469, Article 3). Its scientific part follows EFSA's 2024 guidance, applicable since 1 February 2025: (1) identity of the novel food, (2) production process, (3) compositional data and stability, (4) specifications, (5) history of use of the food and/or its source, (6) proposed uses, use levels and anticipated intake, (7) absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion, (8) toxicological information — genotoxicity and, typically, a subchronic oral toxicity study of at least 90 days (OECD TG 408), with further tiers where triggered, (9) nutritional information and (10) allergenicity. The identity section has specific requirements per category: whole-genome sequences and the absence of viable cells for micro-organisms, cell-line identity and genetic and phenotypic stability for cell cultures. Every study must be notified to EFSA before it starts (Transparency Regulation (EU) 2019/1381), or the application is invalid.

Labelling

Novel foods carry the general mandatory particulars of Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 plus the designation and any additional labelling requirements in their Union-list entry — for insects a statement on allergies to crustaceans and dust mites, for rapeseed protein a warning for people allergic to mustard, for apple cell-culture supplements an age limit. Names can be restricted by other legislation: Regulation (EU) 2026/1739 reserves meat terms such as "chicken" or "steak" for products containing meat and excludes their use for food from cell or tissue culture.

Frontier 2024–2026

The first EU application for cultivated meat (duck cells, July 2024) was still pending in September 2026, while Singapore approved the same company's product in April 2026. Precision-fermented dairy proteins and gas-fermentation biomass are queuing too. Every first opinion on a new class of food sets the evidence standard for all later applicants — watch the Commission's list of application summaries.

The decision rules as logic

A legal test can be written as Boolean logic: each condition is a variable that is either true (1) or false (0), and the rule combines them with "and" (\(\wedge\)), "or" (\(\vee\)) and "not" (\(\neg\)). Writing the rules this way exposes what the navigator does — and where judgement enters, because the variables themselves (what is "significant"?) are decided on evidence. The mini-calculators use 1 for true and 0 for false.

Scope of the Regulation(N1)
\[ S = \neg G \;\wedge\; \neg\,(U_{\text{enz}} \vee U_{\text{add}} \vee U_{\text{flav}} \vee U_{\text{solv}}) \]

a product is within the scope of the novel food regulation if it is not a genetically modified food and is not used solely as a food enzyme, additive, flavouring or extraction solvent.

SymbolMeaningLegal source
\(G\)GM food within the scope of Regulation (EC) No 1829/2003 (contains, consists of or is produced from GMOs)Art. 2(2)(a)
\(U_{\text{enz}},\ U_{\text{add}},\ U_{\text{flav}},\ U_{\text{solv}}\)used as a food enzyme, additive, flavouring or extraction solventArt. 2(2)(b)(i)–(iv)
\(S\)within the scope–
From legal text to logic
  1. Article 2(2): "This Regulation does not apply to: (a) genetically modified foods falling within the scope of Regulation (EC) No 1829/2003; (b) foods when and in so far as they are used as" enzymes, additives, flavourings or extraction solvents.
  2. "Does not apply to A or B" is \(\neg(A \vee B)\), which by De Morgan's law equals \(\neg A \wedge \neg B\).
  3. "When and in so far as" makes \(U\) depend on the use: the same substance can be an additive in one product (out of scope) and a food ingredient in another (in scope).
Assumptions and validity

A food produced with a GM micro-organism but containing no GM material has \(G = 0\). Whether GM material remains is a factual question for the applicant's analyses.

Boolean variables: 1 = true, 0 = false; \(\wedge\) = product, \(\neg x = 1 - x\) ✓
Definition of a novel food(N2)
\[ N = S \wedge \neg H \wedge \big(C_{\text{i}} \vee C_{\text{ii}} \vee \dots \vee C_{\text{x}}\big) \]\[ H = H_0 \wedge \neg (P \wedge \Sigma) \wedge \neg (K \wedge O) \]

a product is a novel food if it is within the scope, has no history of significant consumption in the Union before 15 May 1997, and falls into at least one category. The history is lost if a new process significantly changes the food (category vii) or if a food known only from supplements moves into other foods (category x).

SymbolMeaning
\(H_0\)the food (or its source, as used) was consumed to a significant degree in the Union before 15 May 1997
\(P,\ \Sigma\)a production process not used before that date; significant change in composition or structure
\(K,\ O\)the history is exclusively in food supplements; the food is now intended for other foods
\(C_k\)the food falls into category \(k\); \(C_{\text{vii}} = P\wedge\Sigma\), \(C_{\text{x}} = K \wedge O\)
From legal text to logic
  1. Article 3(2)(a): "novel food means any food that was not used for human consumption to a significant degree within the Union before 15 May 1997 … and that falls under at least one of the following categories". Two conditions joined by "and": \(\neg H \wedge \bigvee_k C_k\).
  2. For a food produced by a new process with significant changes, the food as produced has no history, even if its raw material has: the history term is multiplied by \(\neg(P\wedge\Sigma)\) and category (vii) applies.
  3. The same reasoning applies to category (x): a supplement-only history does not cover other food uses.
  4. Example — UV-treated mushrooms: \(H_0 = 1\), \(P = \Sigma = 1\) ⇒ \(H = 0\), \(C_{\text{vii}} = 1\) ⇒ \(N = 1\). UV-treated skimmed milk: \(\Sigma = 0\) ⇒ \(H = 1\) ⇒ \(N = 0\).
Assumptions and validity

"Significant degree" and "significant change" are not numerical thresholds; they are decided case by case on evidence, ultimately by consultation (Article 4) or implementing act (Article 5). The Boolean form shows the structure of the test, not how to decide each variable.

An excluded product never reaches the history question; a listed food never needs the traditional-food test. Implemented as an ordered chain of if statements, the rule is exactly the path the token follows in the tree.

Investigation tasks

  1. Nineteen cases, one score

    Classify every product in the case library before revealing it. For each wrong answer, identify the node of the tree where your reasoning went wrong (scope, history, change, category, Union list or route). Which node caused most mistakes, and why?

  2. What is a "significant" change?

    UV-treated skimmed milk (vitamin D₃ < 0.375 µg per 100 g) was found not novel; UV-treated mushrooms (up to 10 µg D₂ per 100 g) are novel; UV-treated wine is not novel up to 3 kJ L⁻¹ but novel up to 6 kJ L⁻¹. Propose a transparent criterion for "significant change" (for example, a change larger than the natural variability of the food, or than a nutrient reference value). Would an LED recipe that doubles the vitamin C of basil in a vertical farm be novel under your criterion?

  3. Your own product

    Walk your course-project product through the navigator. Justify every answer with evidence you could show to a control authority, then export the strategy and add it to your report. What would change if one uncertain answer turned out differently?

  4. Wolffia and Lemna

    Fresh Wolffia plants were authorised as a traditional food; Lemna plants and a Lemna protein concentrate went through full applications. Using Eq. N4 and N6, list the conditions that decide which route is available and which is attractive. Which conditions can the applicant influence?

  5. Beat the data protection

    A competitor wants to sell partially defatted cricket powder before 24 January 2028. Using the Authorisation timeline simulator, estimate the probability that an own Article 10 application submitted on 1 October 2026 is authorised before that date. Is it worth it?

  6. The GMO boundary

    Load the precision-fermented β-lactoglobulin and switch the genetic-modification answer from "made with" to "contains / from GMO". Describe what changes legally (regime, EFSA panel, labelling) and what analytical evidence decides between the two answers in practice.

Assumptions and simplifications

  • The tree implements the structure of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 as of September 2026. It cannot decide the factual questions — whether consumption was "significant", whether a change is "significant", whether GM material remains. Those are decided on evidence by the operator, the Member States (consultation), EFSA and the Commission.
  • One main category is selected; real foods can fall into several (the navigator adds (vii) automatically when a new process with significant change is ticked). The traditional-food route is closed if any excluded category applies.
  • "Listed, other specification or use" leads to an application to update the Union list; minor changes may need fewer data (IR 2017/2469, Art. 3(4)). Transitional measures for foods lawfully on the market before 2018 (Art. 35) are not modelled.
  • Indicative timelines come from the stochastic model of the Authorisation timeline simulator; the dossier-quality setting changes clock stops, response times and failure probabilities. They are statistics of past procedures, not predictions for a specific dossier.
  • Case outcomes were verified against the implementing acts on EUR-Lex, the Commission's Union-list pages, its list of consultation outcomes and its summaries of applications (September 2026). The case descriptions are simplified; specifications and conditions of use in the Union list are binding.
  • This is a teaching tool, not legal advice. Before investing in a dossier, check the current Union list and consultation outcomes and seek advice from the competent authority (in Sweden, Livsmedelsverket).
