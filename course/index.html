Future Food Production

The technologies shaping the future of food production — controlled environment agriculture, data-driven cultivation, precision farming and alternative proteins — and the physics, chemistry, biology, society and law that decide whether they work. The selection of technologies is continuously renewed.

Aim

Why this course

Producing food for a growing population within the planetary boundaries is among the most pressing sustainability challenges of our time, and a new generation of technologies is changing what is possible. The aim of the course is to provide students with knowledge of these innovations and, through laboratory and project-based teaching in which they grow, measure and evaluate their own systems, to strengthen their ability to analyse, design and assess emerging food production systems in terms of sustainability, resilience and societal acceptance. The course also provides insight into the factors that enable or hinder the path of new food technologies to the market, knowledge that is central to the professional practice of an environmental engineer.

Content

What you will study

At the core lies controlled environment agriculture — hydroponics, aquaponics and aeroponics — and how physics, chemistry and biology interact in a working system. The course further addresses sensors, artificial intelligence and data-driven models for monitoring and optimising production, precision agriculture and remote sensing, alternative protein production, and consumer acceptance and the EU regulation on novel foods as decisive for the uptake of new technologies.

In the course project, you grow, monitor and evaluate your own soilless cultivation system and investigate consumer acceptance of novel foods in a sensory study.

    Project · LO1–LO7

    Project report and presentation

    Group report (IMRaD) on the soilless-cultivation experiment and sensory study, with statistics, resource-use indicators and model fitting on your own data; poster or oral presentation; individual contribution statement. See the assessment rubric.

    Laboratories · LO2 · LO3 · LO5

    Laboratory assignments

    Selected investigations from the virtual laboratories (e.g., lighting design, nutrient recipe, aquaponic balance, climate control), documented with saved investigations and short written answers.

    Written exam · LO1 · LO2 · LO3 · LO6 · LO7

    Individual written examination

    Calculations (mass, energy, water and light balances; efficiency indicators), explanations of system principles, and an analysis of a novel technology's sustainability, resilience, acceptance and regulatory pathway. Practise with the problem generator.
